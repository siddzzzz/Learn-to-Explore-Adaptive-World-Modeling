## 1. Problem Formulation: The Epistemic POMDP

We formalize an agent interacting with an unfamiliar, rule-governed world as an **Epistemic Partially Observable Markov Decision Process** with unobserved structural dynamics parameters:

$$\mathcal{M} = \langle \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \Theta, \mathcal{T}_\theta, \Omega, \mathcal{O}, \gamma \rangle$$

where:
- $\mathcal{S} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ is the underlying state space.
- $\mathcal{A}$ is the action space (which may include physical movements and atomic experimental interventions $\text{do}(X = x)$).
- $\Theta$ is the hypothesis space representing latent structural mechanisms, causal directed acyclic graphs (DAGs), or physical parameterizations.
- $\theta^* \in \Theta$ denotes the true, unobserved ground-truth rule governing the environment.
- $\mathcal{T}_{\theta^*}: \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A} \to \Delta(\mathcal{S})$ represents the true transition dynamics parameterized by $\theta^*$:
  $$s_{t+1} \sim \mathcal{T}_{\theta^*}(\cdot \mid s_t, a_t)$$
- $\Omega$ is the observation space, and $\mathcal{O}: \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A} \to \Delta(\Omega)$ is the observation emission distribution:
  $$o_t \sim \mathcal{O}(\cdot \mid s_t, a_t)$$
- $\gamma \in [0, 1)$ is the discount factor.

In the pure scientific discovery phase, **no extrinsic task reward is provided** ($R_{\text{task}} \equiv 0$). The agent's sole purpose is to design and execute a sequence of actions $\tau = (a_0, a_1, \dots, a_{H-1})$ that yields maximum information regarding the true rule $\theta^*$.

## 2. Derivation of the Information-Theoretic Objective

### 2.1 Bayesian Experimental Design (BED)
Following Lindley (1956) and Chaloner & Verdinelli (1995), the utility of an experiment (or trajectory of experiments) $\tau$ starting from initial state $s_0$ is defined as the **Expected Information Gain (EIG)** about the unknown parameter $\theta^*$:

$$\mathcal{U}_{\text{EIG}}(\tau \mid s_0) = I(\Theta; \mathcal{Y} \mid s_0, \tau)$$

where:
- $\mathcal{Y} = (o_1, o_2, \dots, o_H)$ is the random variable of future observations generated by executing $\tau$ under the environment dynamics.
- $I(\Theta; \mathcal{Y} \mid s_0, \tau)$ is the Shannon Mutual Information between the latent hypothesis $\Theta$ and the empirical outcome trajectory $\mathcal{Y}$.

By definition of mutual information:
$$I(\Theta; \mathcal{Y} \mid s_0, \tau) = \mathcal{H}(\Theta) - \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{Y} \sim p(\mathcal{Y} \mid s_0, \tau)} \left[ \mathcal{H}(\Theta \mid \mathcal{Y}) \right]$$

Equivalently, by the symmetric property of mutual information:
$$I(\Theta; \mathcal{Y} \mid s_0, \tau) = \mathcal{H}(\mathcal{Y} \mid s_0, \tau) - \mathbb{E}_{\theta \sim p(\theta)} \left[ \mathcal{H}(\mathcal{Y} \mid s_0, \tau, \theta) \right]$$

### 2.2 Transition from Myopic to Trajectory-Level Information Gain
Standard active learning (e.g., BALD; Houlsby et al., 2011) evaluates a single one-step query. However, in physical and causal worlds, testing a hypothesis requires **multi-step sequential setup** (e.g., navigating to an apparatus, configuring reagents, and then triggering a reaction).

Let $\tau_{0:t} = (s_0, a_0, s_1, \dots, s_t)$ be the history up to time $t$, and let $b_t(\theta) = p(\theta \mid \tau_{0:t})$ be the agent's posterior belief. For a planned horizon $H$, the cumulative trajectory EIG is:

$$\mathcal{U}_{\text{EIG}}(\pi \mid b_t, s_t) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi} \left[ \sum_{k=0}^{H-1} \gamma^k r_{\text{epistemic}}(s_{t+k}, a_{t+k}) \right]$$

where $r_{\text{epistemic}}(s, a)$ is the one-step Expected Information Gain at state $s$ taking action $a$.

## 3. Derivation of Contrastive Hypothesis Discrepancy (CHD)

### 3.1 The Failure Mode of Prior Art: The Noisy TV Problem
State-of-the-art model-based exploration algorithms (such as **Plan2Explore**; Sekar et al., 2020) define epistemic reward via the variance of an ensemble of transition models:
$$r_{\text{Plan2Explore}}(s, a) \approx \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M \| f_{\phi_m}(s, a) - \bar{f}(s, a) \|^2$$

**Failure Mode**: If the environment contains inherently stochastic or unpredictable components (aleatoric noise $\xi \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ or a "Noisy TV" screen displaying white noise), each model $f_{\phi_m}$ attempts to fit the noise, leading to persistent non-zero ensemble variance. The agent remains trapped exploring the unmodelable noise.

### 3.2 The Contrastive Hypothesis Discrepancy (CHD) Formulation
Let the agent's belief over candidate hypotheses be represented as a weighted particle set:
$$b_t(\theta) = \sum_{k=1}^K w_k \delta_{\theta^{(k)}}(\theta), \quad \sum_{k=1}^K w_k = 1$$

Under hypothesis $\theta^{(k)}$, the predictive distribution of the next state is $p(s' \mid s, a, \theta^{(k)})$.

The marginal predictive distribution averaged over all hypotheses is:
$$\bar{p}(s' \mid s, a) = \sum_{k=1}^K w_k p(s' \mid s, a, \theta^{(k)})$$

We derive the **Contrastive Hypothesis Discrepancy (CHD)** as the **Generalized Jensen-Shannon Divergence ($D_{\text{JS}}$)** across the hypothesis predictions:

$$r_{\text{CHD}}(s, a) \triangleq D_{\text{JS}}^{w_{1:K}} \left( p(\cdot \mid s, a, \theta^{(1)}), \dots, p(\cdot \mid s, a, \theta^{(K)}) \right)$$

By definition of Jensen-Shannon Divergence:
$$D_{\text{JS}}^{w_{1:K}} = \mathcal{H}(\bar{p}(s' \mid s, a)) - \sum_{k=1}^K w_k \mathcal{H}(p(s' \mid s, a, \theta^{(k)}))$$

### 3.3 Theorem 1 (Aleatoric Invariance / Noisy TV Immunity)
**Theorem**: Let the transition dynamics decompose into a structural deterministic mechanism $g_\theta(s, a)$ and an independent additive aleatoric noise channel $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_{\text{noise}})$ common to the environment:
$$s' = g_{\theta^*}(s, a) + \epsilon$$

Then:
1. If two hypotheses $\theta^{(i)}$ and $\theta^{(j)}$ agree on the structural mechanism at $(s, a)$ (i.e., $g_{\theta^{(i)}}(s, a) = g_{\theta^{(j)}}(s, a)$), then:
   $$r_{\text{CHD}}(s, a) \equiv 0$$
   regardless of the magnitude of the noise covariance $\Sigma_{\text{noise}}$.
2. $r_{\text{CHD}}(s, a) > 0$ strictly if and only if there exist active hypotheses $i, j$ with non-zero weights $w_i, w_j > 0$ such that $g_{\theta^{(i)}}(s, a) \neq g_{\theta^{(j)}}(s, a)$.

**Proof**:
Under Gaussian additive noise, each model predicts:
$$p(s' \mid s, a, \theta^{(k)}) = \mathcal{N}\left( g_{\theta^{(k)}}(s, a), \Sigma_{\text{noise}} \right)$$

The entropy of each individual prediction is identical:
$$\mathcal{H}(p(s' \mid s, a, \theta^{(k)})) = \frac{1}{2} \ln \left| (2\pi e)^d \Sigma_{\text{noise}} \right|$$

Hence:
$$\sum_{k=1}^K w_k \mathcal{H}(p(s' \mid s, a, \theta^{(k)})) = \frac{1}{2} \ln \left| (2\pi e)^d \Sigma_{\text{noise}} \right|$$

If all candidate models agree on the outcome: $g_{\theta^{(1)}}(s, a) = \dots = g_{\theta^{(K)}}(s, a) = \mu$, then the mixture distribution is unimodal Gaussian:
$$\bar{p}(s' \mid s, a) = \sum_{k=1}^K w_k \mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma_{\text{noise}}) = \mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma_{\text{noise}})$$

Therefore:
$$\mathcal{H}(\bar{p}(s' \mid s, a)) = \frac{1}{2} \ln \left| (2\pi e)^d \Sigma_{\text{noise}} \right|$$

Subtracting the two terms yields:
$$r_{\text{CHD}}(s, a) = \frac{1}{2} \ln \left| (2\pi e)^d \Sigma_{\text{noise}} \right| - \frac{1}{2} \ln \left| (2\pi e)^d \Sigma_{\text{noise}} \right| \equiv 0$$

$\blacksquare$

**Significance**: This mathematically guarantees that the agent will **never perform an action merely to observe stochastic noise**. It will ONLY execute actions where competing hypotheses make conflicting predictions.

## 4. Bayesian Belief Updating

When an transition $(s_t, a_t, s_{t+1})$ is observed from the real environment, the belief weights over the hypothesis set are updated via Bayes' rule:

$$w_{t+1}^{(k)} = \frac{w_t^{(k)} \cdot p(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t, \theta^{(k)})}{\sum_{j=1}^K w_t^{(j)} \cdot p(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t, \theta^{(j)})}$$

### Log-Likelihood Form for Numerical Stability:
Let $\ell_k = \ln w_t^{(k)} + \ln p(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t, \theta^{(k)})$. Then:
$$w_{t+1}^{(k)} = \frac{\exp(\ell_k - \max_j \ell_j)}{\sum_{m=1}^K \exp(\ell_m - \max_j \ell_j)}$$

### Epistemic Shannon Entropy of Belief:
At any step $t$, the total uncertainty over which rule governs the world is given by the discrete Shannon Entropy of the belief:
$$\mathcal{H}(b_t) = - \sum_{k=1}^K w_t^{(k)} \ln w_t^{(k)}$$

When $\mathcal{H}(b_t) \to 0$, the agent has resolved all structural ambiguity and identified $\theta^*$ with near certainty.

## 5. Non-Myopic Trajectory Planning

Because finding the state $s$ where hypotheses diverge may require a multi-step navigation sequence, we employ **Trajectory-Level Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)** and **Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI)** control in the world model.

Given current state $s_t$ and belief $b_t$, the planner optimizes an action sequence $A = (a_0, a_1, \dots, a_{H-1})$ over horizon $H$:

$$J(A) = \sum_{h=0}^{H-1} \gamma^h r_{\text{CHD}}(\hat{s}_h, a_h)$$

where imaginary state rollouts evolve under the ensemble belief dynamics:
$$\hat{s}_0 = s_t, \quad \hat{s}_{h+1} \sim \sum_{k=1}^K w^{(k)} p(\cdot \mid \hat{s}_h, a_h, \theta^{(k)})$$

The Cross-Entropy Method iteratively refines a Gaussian distribution over action sequences $\mathcal{N}(\mu_{0:H-1}, \Sigma_{0:H-1})$ by taking the top $E$ elite trajectories:
$$\mu \leftarrow \frac{1}{E} \sum_{e=1}^E A_e^{\text{elite}}, \quad \Sigma \leftarrow \frac{1}{E} \sum_{e=1}^E (A_e^{\text{elite}} - \mu)(A_e^{\text{elite}} - \mu)^\top + \epsilon I$$

The agent executes the first action $a_0^*$, observes $(s_t, a_0^*, s_{t+1})$, updates its Bayesian posterior $b_{t+1}$, and replans in a receding-horizon manner.

## 7. Complete HEx-WM Algorithm

```
Algorithm 1: HEx-WM (Hypothesis-Experimenting World Model)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Input: Hypothesis space Θ = {θ^(1), ..., θ^(K)}, Prior weights w_0 = (1/K, ..., 1/K),
       Exploration budget T_explore, Planning horizon H, Elite count E
Output: Identified rule θ* and converged world model

1: Initialize belief b_0 = (w_0^(1), ..., w_0^(K))
2: Observe initial state s_0 from environment
3: for t = 0 to T_explore - 1 do
4:    // 1. Check if uncertainty is resolved
5:    if Entropy(b_t) < ε_threshold then
6:        break // Exploration complete; rule discovered!
7:    end if
8:
9:    // 2. Trajectory-Level Bayesian Experimental Design (CEM Planner)
10:   Initialize action distribution q(A) = N(0, I_{H × dim(A)})
11:   for iteration = 1 to N_cem_iters do
12:       Sample N_samples candidate trajectories A_1, ..., A_N ~ q(A)
13:       for each candidate A_n = (a_{n,0}, ..., a_{n,H-1}) do
14:           Rollout imagined states ŝ_{n,0} = s_t, ..., ŝ_{n,H} using belief dynamics
15:           Compute trajectory EIG: J(A_n) = ∑_{h=0}^{H-1} γ^h · r_CHD(ŝ_{n,h}, a_{n,h})
16:       end for
17:       Select top E elite trajectories with highest J(A_n)
18:       Refine q(A) using mean and covariance of elites
19:   end for
20:
21:   // 3. Execute first action of optimal experiment
22:   a_t = mean(q(A))[0]
23:   Step environment: s_{t+1} ~ Env.step(a_t)
24:
25:   // 4. Exact / Variational Bayesian Belief Update
26:   for k = 1 to K do
27:       w_{t+1}^(k) ∝ w_t^(k) · p(s_{t+1} | s_t, a_t, θ^(k))
28:   end for
29:   Normalize weights w_{t+1}
30:   b_{t+1} = (w_{t+1}^(1), ..., w_{t+1}^(K))
31: end for
32: Return identified rule θ_hat = argmax_k w_T^(k)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```
